## What Shooked is

Shooked shows you public earthquake information and helps you tell people you're safe afterwards. It is an **informational and convenience tool**.

## What Shooked is not

**Shooked is not an emergency service and is not an earthquake early-warning system.**

- It does **not** warn you before shaking arrives. Earthquake data is published by the USGS *after* an event is detected, typically minutes later.
- It does **not** replace official warning systems such as ShakeAlert, AFAD, J-Alert, or your local emergency authority.
- It does **not** replace calling emergency services. In an emergency, contact your local emergency number.

Never rely on Shooked to keep yourself or anyone else safe.

## Accuracy

Earthquake data comes from the U.S. Geological Survey and is presented as received. Magnitudes, locations, depths and impact estimates are **preliminary and are frequently revised**, sometimes substantially. Shooked does not verify, correct, or guarantee this data, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the USGS.

Distance calculations are approximate and depend on your device's location accuracy.

## Check-ins are a person's own statement, not a verified fact

This is the most important thing to understand about Shooked.

When someone sends a check-in, they are making a personal statement about themselves at that moment. Shooked passes that statement along. **Shooked does not verify it, cannot verify it, and does not represent it as true.**

Specifically:

- **A check-in is not confirmation that anyone is safe.** It reflects what one person chose to say at one moment. It may be sent mistakenly, sent by someone else holding the phone, sent before the person understood their situation, or sent and then overtaken by events.
- **A check-in describes a moment, not a condition.** Someone who reported being safe at 3:00 may not be safe at 3:30. Aftershocks, fires, gas leaks and structural failures all follow earthquakes. **Never treat a past check-in as current information.**
- **The absence of a check-in means nothing.** A person may be perfectly fine with a dead battery, no signal, a phone left at home, or simply no thought of the app. **Do not conclude anything from silence.**
- **Shooked cannot confirm a message arrived.** iOS does not report SMS delivery to apps, and push notifications can fail. The app tells you that *you sent* a check-in; it never claims anyone received or read it.

**Do not use Shooked to decide whether someone needs help.** If you are worried about somebody, contact them directly and contact emergency services. A check-in is a courtesy, not a welfare check, and not a substitute for either.

## Message delivery is not guaranteed

Check-in messages depend on things outside our control: your cellular or internet connection, Apple's Messages service, your carrier, your recipients' devices and settings, and — for app-to-app check-ins — push notification delivery. **Messages may be delayed, or may not arrive at all.** Do not treat a sent check-in as confirmation that anyone received it.
