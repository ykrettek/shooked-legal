# Shooked — Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 6 July 2026_

Shooked is built so that the sensitive parts of your life stay on your phone. This policy describes exactly what the app touches and where it goes.

## The short version

- Your family contacts are stored **only on your device**. We never upload them.
- Your location is used **on your device** to find nearby earthquakes. It is never sent to us.
- We have **no accounts, no login, and no email address on file**.
- We do **not** use analytics, advertising, or third-party trackers.

## What Shooked stores on your device

**Family contacts.** Names and phone numbers you add for check-ins are saved in the app's local storage. They are not transmitted to Shooked's servers, backed up to our systems, or shared with anyone. Deleting the app deletes them.

**Your settings.** Magnitude and time filters, unit preference, and alert locations you create.

**An anonymous device identifier.** A random ID generated on first launch. It is not derived from your phone number, Apple ID, email, or any hardware identifier, and it cannot be traced back to you.

## What leaves your device

**Earthquake data requests.** Shooked downloads public earthquake data from the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://earthquake.usgs.gov) and public fault-line map data. These are ordinary web requests. We do not attach your identity or location to them.

**Check-in messages you choose to send.** When you tap "I'm Safe":

- For contacts reached by text message, the message is composed in Apple's Messages app and sent **from your own phone number**, by you, after you press Send. Shooked cannot send a text on your behalf — iOS does not permit it — and the message never passes through our systems.
- For family members linked through Shooked, your anonymous device identifier and the message text are sent to Shooked's servers solely to deliver that notification to the people you linked with. We do not retain message content after delivery.

**Did You Feel It? reports.** If you choose to submit an earthquake report, the app opens the official USGS form in your browser. Anything you enter goes directly to USGS under [their privacy policy](https://www.usgs.gov/policies/privacy-policies). Shooked does not see or store it.

## Location

Shooked requests location access to:

- Show earthquakes near you and calculate distance
- Decide whether an earthquake is close enough to be worth alerting you about
- Name the specific earthquake in your check-in message
- Add a map link to a help request, only if you choose to include it

Your location is processed **on your device**. It is not transmitted to Shooked or to any third party. The only time your location leaves your phone is in a help request you choose to send: if you tap **I need help** and choose **Send with my location**, a map link to your position is added to the text message you send. It goes only to the people you send it to, from your own phone, and never to Shooked. You can always choose to send without your location. You can decline location access and still use the app — you will lose distance readouts and nearby-quake alerts, but check-in and the map still work.

## Notifications

Notification permission is used to alert you about earthquakes near you and, if you have linked family members, to receive their check-ins. You can turn notifications off at any time in iOS Settings.

## Children

Shooked is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

## Data we do not collect

We do not collect your name, email address, contacts list, photos, browsing history, advertising identifiers, or device fingerprints. There is no analytics SDK and no advertising SDK in the app.

## Your choices

- **Remove a contact** — delete it in the app; it is erased from your device immediately.
- **Revoke location or notifications** — iOS Settings → Shooked.
- **Delete everything** — deleting the app removes all local data, including contacts, settings, and the anonymous identifier.

## Changes

If this policy changes materially, the updated version will be published here with a new date, and significant changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
